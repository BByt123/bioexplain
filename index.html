Light excites electrons

When chlorophyll absorbs a photon, an electron briefly moves to a higher energy state. In a photosystem, an energized electron can then pass to an acceptor. Electron transport helps the chloroplast make ATP and NADPH.

Heat changes enzyme activity

In the stroma, enzymes such as Rubisco help the Calvin cycle fix CO2. Warming can speed these reactions up to an optimum. Above it, activity falls. Extreme heat can alter an enzyme's shape and reduce its ability to work.

Two different jobs

What changes when you turn up the light or raise the temperature?

More light
At the same wavelength, brighter light means more photons arrive each second. In darkness, chlorophyll stops absorbing light energy.
More warmth
Molecules move faster as temperature rises. Enzyme activity can increase up to an optimum and then decline. Warming alone cannot sustain photosynthesis in darkness.
How they connect
Light-dependent reactions in the thylakoid membrane supply ATP and NADPH. The Calvin cycle uses them to turn carbon dioxide into compounds the plant can use to make sugars.
